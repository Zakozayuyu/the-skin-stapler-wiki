The Skin Stapler is Elias Crow, the owner of the Flesh Pit barbecue restaurant in Carrion City. He becomes the killer because Dick Slater killed his twin brother, Silus Kaine. The identity reveal and revenge motive are confirmed by multiple independent full-game playthroughs, and the ending is covered separately on the [ending explained page](/ending).

## Who is the Skin Stapler?

The Skin Stapler's real name is **Elias Crow**. He runs the Flesh Pit, a barbecue restaurant in Carrion City, which is why the final chapter uses that location name. Two independent complete-game transcripts independently confirm the same identity reveal: the investigation uncovers that Elias and Silus Kaine were twins separated at birth, one raised by the Ken family and the other by the Crow family.

The reveal is not a fan theory or demo-era guess. Both CaseOh's full-game playthrough and GabeHype's 100% run contain the same origin dialogue and the same identification of Elias Crow as the Skin Stapler. The character announcement, Steam page, and developer materials never name the killer before the player reaches the late-game investigation.

## Why does Elias Crow kill?

Elias kills because Dick Slater killed his brother, **Silus Kaine**. The game's investigation dialogue states that Slater was responsible for Silus's death, and Elias tells Slater directly that he took someone important from him. The murders are Elias's answer to that loss: he targets people connected to the city and stalks them through their homes and workplaces.

The motive is personal rather than random. Elias does not kill strangers for sport — he kills because of what happened to Silus, and he pursues Slater specifically as the person responsible. That connection drives the final confrontation at the Flesh Pit.

## How is the identity revealed?

The identity unfolds through the investigation rather than a single cutscene. Early clues include a suspect file naming Silus Kaine and the Flesh Pit advertisement seen earlier in the game. As Slater and Robbie Knox piece together the case, the dialogue exposes the twin origin: one child goes to the Ken family and is named Silus, the other goes to the Crow family and is named Elias.

The direct confirmation arrives when the detectives connect the name Elias Crow to the Flesh Pit owner. Full-game dialogue states: "Elias Crow is the owner of the Flesh Pit. It's the best barbecue in town." The motive then clicks into place: "Dick, you killed his brother. And now Elias is out for revenge."

The route uses several recordings rather than one isolated clue. A VHS recovered from Luna’s crime scene, the tape Scaroline finds at Underworld VHS, a late “Come find me, detective” tape, Dick’s private recording, and an older account of the twins all help assemble the story. The exact numbered chapter for each recording is inferred from the recorded route because the developer has not published an official numbered chapter list.

## The Flesh Pit connection

Elias's public identity as a barbecue restaurant owner is what makes the final location significant. The Flesh Pit is not just a random setting — it is Elias's own establishment, which explains why the investigation leads there and why the final confrontation takes place in that specific location.

The name "Flesh Pit" also carries a double meaning within the game's grindhouse tone: a barbecue restaurant whose name happens to describe what Elias does to his victims. The official Itch.io page confirms that the Skin Stapler skins victims and staples their flesh to nearby walls, and the Flesh Pit name ties that violence to Elias's public life.

## What the investigation uncovers

The detectives find files listing Silus Kaine as a suspect with no known surviving family. That detail is the first hint that the case is more personal than it appears. As the investigation progresses, the twin origin story emerges through dialogue and discovered records.

The evidence trail also connects the distorted audio tapes left at crime scenes to the larger pattern. The tapes taunt police and point to something deeper beneath the city, and the final reveal shows that "something deeper" is Elias's personal vendetta against Slater.

## Is the Skin Stapler the same person as the killer?

Yes. "The Skin Stapler" is the name the media and police use for the serial killer. The killer's real identity is Elias Crow. The name "Skin Stapler" comes from the method: victims are skinned and their flesh is stapled to nearby walls. The Steam page, Itch.io description, and multiple gameplay transcripts all confirm this method.

## Elias Crow and Silus Kaine: the twin origin

The game reveals that Elias Crow and Silus Kaine were twins separated at birth. One child was placed with the Ken family and given the name Silus. The other went to the Crow family and was named Elias. This origin is stated in late-game dialogue and confirmed by both major full-game transcripts.

The spelling of the surname varies across sources — "Kaine," "Kane," and "Kain" all appear in different transcripts and references. The exact spelling in the final game credits has not been independently confirmed from a screenshot, so this guide uses "Kaine" as the most common variant while noting the inconsistency.

## What happens to Elias?

Dick shoots Elias during the final confrontation after Robbie’s sacrifice. The available footage does not clearly establish whether Elias dies at the scene or is arrested afterward, and one streamer’s closing comment conflicts with a simple death reading. This page therefore treats the gunshot as confirmed and Elias’s final legal or medical status as unresolved. The [ending explained page](/ending) covers the wider finale.
