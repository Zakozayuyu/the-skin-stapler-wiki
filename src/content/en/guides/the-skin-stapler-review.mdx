## What kind of game is being reviewed?

The Skin Stapler is a short, story-driven horror game set in 1984 Carrion City, a town the review calls rotten and populated by absolute weirdos. You play alternating viewpoints: burned-out detective Dick Slater working the investigation, and the stalker perspective during the killing-spree chapters. The publisher's own pitch — hunt a serial killer through a short, story-driven horror nightmare — is the frame the review works inside.

Fourteen short chapters fill those 2.5 hours. Nine are playable location chapters such as the Burrows Apartments, the Quick Fix, and Lazar's Blood Bank, and the rest are Aftermath interludes that pull each victim's story back into Slater's case file. The [full walkthrough](/walkthrough) lists the chapter route with timestamps.

## What the review praises

The victim chapters are the standout. The reviewer calls the Fefe apartment opening the most outright tense chapter in the game, and the remaining victim sequences keep that edge: ordinary workplace shifts — serving hot dogs, filling donuts, rewinding tapes — that the player knows a masked figure is closing in on. That dramatic irony is the design's best trick, and the review singles it out as the favorite part of the package.

Presentation earns the second round of praise. The VHS-style FMV sequences, the synthesizer-heavy retro score, and the fully voiced cast give the game its grindhouse identity. The review also enjoys the writing: Carrion City is deliberately packed with grotesque oddballs, and the game expects one last laugh before you die, per the publisher's own tagline.

## What the review criticizes

Both play styles are light on gameplay. The investigation chapters reduce to walking small areas and completing tasks, and the killing-spree chapters lean on short interaction loops. The reviewer's phrase is that the mechanics feel a touch repetitive — the game runs 2.5 hours, and the interaction vocabulary does not grow much across those 14 chapters.

That criticism is the main reason the verdict lands on acquired taste rather than universal recommendation. The review treats the thin mechanics as the cost of the game's humor and pacing rather than a failure of it, but it does not pretend the systems are deep. Numbers do not settle the taste question — a million views of a hot dog scene is not a review — but they do show which moments audiences respond to. The [Reddit round-up](/guides/the-skin-stapler-reddit) tracks that discussion in detail.

## How to decide in three steps

1. **Play the free demo first.** It packages three sections — the police station, the Quick Fix, and Gutters End — and its reception before launch was strong enough that the publisher led with it. If the demo's jokes land and its pace feels right, the full game is more of exactly that.
2. **Watch one victim chapter.** A few minutes of a workplace chapter tells you whether the tension-plus-comedy mix works for you. The CaseOh and Joe Bart videos are the fastest way to sample the tone without spoilers for the finale.
3. **Buy for the story, not the systems.** At 2.5 hours and a launch price of 14.99 euros on Steam, the value case is a one-sitting grindhouse story with full voice acting — not a mechanics sandbox. Decide with that framing and the review's criticisms stop mattering as much.

## Is The Skin Stapler worth it for horror fans?

For players who already like PSX-style throwbacks, short-form horror, and joke-forward slasher writing, the review's answer is yes. The victim chapters deliver genuine tension, the FMV presentation lands, and 14 chapters keep the joke rate high enough to carry the short runtime.

For players who want systems — deep investigation tools, combat, branching choices — the review's answer is that this game does not offer them, and no update promise changes that. The single storyline ends at the Flesh Pit, which the [ending guide](/ending) covers in full.
