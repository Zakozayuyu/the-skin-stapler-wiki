The Skin Stapler tells one continuous story: in 1984, burned-out detective Dick Slater hunts a serial killer through Carrion City while the game cuts between the killer's victims — and the killer himself. The killer is Elias Crow, owner of the Flesh Pit barbecue restaurant, and his motive is revenge: Slater killed his twin brother, Silus Kain. The story runs about 2.5 to 4 hours across 14 short chapters and has exactly one ending.

**Heavy spoilers below.** This page covers the full plot, including the identity reveal and the finale. The spoiler-free basics live on the [gameplay guide](/guides/the-skin-stapler-gameplay), and the finale itself has a dedicated [ending explained page](/ending).

## The premise: a city with a skin-stapling problem

The official store premise sets the story in motion with a grim pattern: victims are skinned, their flesh is stapled to nearby walls, and distorted audio tapes left at the scenes taunt the police. Slater, a burned-out detective, draws the case, and the game's hook is that you do not only play the investigation — you also spend chapters inside the lives of the people being followed.

That dual structure is the story's engine. One chapter puts you behind Slater's desk at the Carrion City Police Department; the next puts you in an apartment, a convenience store, or a bowling alley as the stalker closes in. The Aftermath interludes then fold each victim's story back into the case file, so the two halves keep tightening around each other until the investigation names a suspect.

## How the 14 chapters tell the story

The playable route runs: the Burrows Apartments opening with Fefe, the Carrion City Police Department with Slater, the Quick Fix with Hexy, Gutters End Bowling Alley with Blair, Luna's donut shop, Underworld VHS with Scaroline, and Lazar's Blood Bank with Nurse Layna. Four Aftermath interludes sit between those location chapters, and the Final Aftermath connects the investigation chapters to the endgame. Closing In follows, and The Flesh Pit closes the story.

Steam's achievement names confirm that exact chapter list, and the chaptered reference walkthrough puts timestamps on it — the Burrows Apartments at 00:03:06 through to The Flesh Pit at 02:34:00. The [full walkthrough](/walkthrough) maps the route with those timestamps.

## The killer's identity: Elias Crow

The investigation builds to a single reveal: the Skin Stapler is Elias Crow, the public-facing owner of the Flesh Pit, billed in-game as the best barbecue in town. The name is hidden in plain sight — the Flesh Pit advertisement appears early, and late-game dialogue states directly that Elias Crow owns the restaurant.

The reveal lands the way it does because the game plants the pieces across viewpoints. Suspect files, the Flesh Pit ad, crime-scene tapes, and the Aftermath segments each hold one fragment, and the detectives assemble them on screen rather than in a single exposition dump. The dedicated [who is the Skin Stapler page](/guides/who-is-the-skin-stapler) tracks every clue in the chain.

## The twin origin and the motive

The motive is the story's emotional spine: Slater killed Elias's twin brother, Silus Kain, and Elias builds his killing spree around making Slater feel an equivalent loss. Late-game dialogue says it plainly — Dick, you killed his brother, and now Elias is out for revenge.

An orphanage recording, delivered in Father Omani's voice, explains how two brothers ended up on opposite sides of the same case: the twins were separated at birth, one placed with the Ken family and named Silus, the other placed with the Crow family and named Elias. Automated captions spell the surname as Kane or Ken in places; this site standardizes on Kain, the spelling used across the walkthrough and ending pages.

## How the tapes drive the plot

The distorted audio tapes are the story's connective tissue. The killer leaves them at crime scenes to taunt the police, and repairing and reviewing them becomes a running investigation task — one Steam achievement, Jank Ya Later!, even unlocks specifically for fixing the Skin Stapler tapes. The tapes also carry the story's deepest clue: the orphanage recording that reveals the twin origin.

Individual chapters feed the tape thread too. A VHS recovered from Luna's crime scene matters to the case, and Scaroline's Underworld VHS chapter puts you inside the rental store where tape handling is part of the job. The [tapes guide](/guides/the-skin-stapler-all-tapes) follows every recording through the route.

## The ending: one confrontation at the Flesh Pit

The story has one ending, and two independent full-game playthroughs confirm the same sequence. Slater traces the killer to the Flesh Pit, his own restaurant. Elias gets the upper hand first: Robbie Knox is struck down, and Elias presses Slater to pick up Robbie's gun. Slater does, shoots Elias, and leaves Carrion City. There is no moral menu, no branching dialogue, and no alternate finale — the investigation funnels into a single confrontation.

The gunshot resolves the revenge plot on screen, while what happens to Elias afterward — death at the scene or arrest — is left unstated by the footage checked for the [ending guide](/ending). Either way, the detective walks out of the city, and the story ends where the premise began: Slater, the case, and a city that took everything from both brothers.

## How the story is actually told

The storytelling leans on the game's grindhouse package: VHS-style FMV sequences, a fully voiced cast of ten credited performers, low-poly PSX-era environments, and a synthesizer score. Dialogue carries most of the plot, the Aftermath interludes carry the victims' perspectives, and the tapes carry the killer's voice. Nothing in the story requires reading outside the game — every reveal arrives through playable scenes or recordings you handle yourself.

Review coverage of the story side is consistent with that design: the victim chapters are called the most outright tense material in the game, and the 1984 setting full of grotesque oddballs is treated as the story's flavor rather than set dressing.

## How to experience the story in order

1. **Play the location chapters in route order.** The game is linear — Burrows Apartments, CCPD, Quick Fix, Gutters End, Luna's shop, Underworld VHS, Lazar's Blood Bank — and each chapter's ending flows directly into the next. Chapter select exists for replays, but the story is written for a first run in order.
2. **Handle every tape interaction.** Repaired tapes are plot devices first and achievements second. The orphanage recording and the crime-scene tapes are the story's two biggest reveals, and both arrive through the tape mechanic.
3. **Do not skip the Aftermath interludes.** The four Aftermath parts and the Final Aftermath are where the victim chapters connect to Slater's case. Skipping them in a replay breaks the story's throughline — the route's chapter list shows exactly where each one sits.

## Does the story have multiple endings or secret routes?

No. One main storyline ending is the confirmed structure. Community talk about epilogue variations tied to which tapes you hear exists, but matched runs demonstrating different endings have not surfaced — two complete playthroughs of the game show the same finale. The story is a straight line from the Burrows Apartments to the Flesh Pit, and its replay value sits in the viewpoints and jokes rather than branching plot.

## The Skin Stapler story FAQ

**Q: Who is the Skin Stapler in the story?**
A: Elias Crow, the owner of the Flesh Pit barbecue restaurant. The investigation reveals his identity in the late game, and the finale confronts him at the restaurant.

**Q: Why does the Skin Stapler kill his victims?**
A: Revenge. Slater killed his twin brother Silus Kain, and Elias stages his murders as an answer — skinned victims, stapled flesh, and taunting tapes aimed at the police.

**Q: How long is the story?**
A: About 2.5 hours for a direct run across 14 short chapters; recorded runs with fuller exploration run closer to 3 hours, and slower first playthroughs can approach 4.

**Q: Does Slater catch the killer?**
A: Yes. The story ends at the Flesh Pit, where Slater shoots Elias after Robbie is struck down, then leaves Carrion City. The [ending page](/ending) covers the scene beat by beat.

**Q: Is the story connected to the demo?**
A: The demo's three sections — the police station, the Quick Fix, and Gutters End — are the story's second, third, and fourth chapters in shortened form. The full game starts from the beginning with Fefe's apartment.

The short version: one detective, one vengeful twin, one city full of weirdos, and one ending that closes the file. Every stop on that route has its own page in this wiki if you want the scene-level detail.
