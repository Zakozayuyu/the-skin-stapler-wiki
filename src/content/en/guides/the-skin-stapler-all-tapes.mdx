The Skin Stapler tapes are the killer's voice in the story: distorted audio recordings left at crime scenes to taunt the police, plus the investigation recordings that piece the case together. You collect, repair, and review tapes throughout the route, and one Steam achievement — Runebee's Jank Ya Later! — unlocks specifically for fixing the Skin Stapler tapes. The game never shows a total tape counter, so the exact count of repairable tapes is a detail to verify in-game rather than a number any page can promise.

Six rows, three jobs: the tapes are atmosphere, evidence, and plot device at once. The sections below walk through each one and show where the tape work sits in the chapter route.

## The taunt tapes: the killer talks to the police

The official premise is explicit: victims are skinned and stapled to walls, and distorted audio tapes left at the scenes taunt the police. Those recordings give the killer a voice without ever showing him — the early-game audio is deliberately grotesque, and it frames the investigation as a conversation the killer is having with Slater through crime scenes.

The taunt tapes also set the story's tone. Reviewers flag the game's dark comedy as a defining trait, and the tape audio is where that register is loudest: the killer performs for the investigators, and the distorted VHS-era delivery makes the jokes land colder.

## The investigation tapes: the case file in audio form

Beyond the taunts, the route's repair-and-review loop turns tapes into evidence. Damaged tapes surface across the chapters, and the game requires you to fix or review them before the investigation advances — Steam's achievement list names the activity directly, and full-game footage shows Slater handling tape repair during the late-game stretch at DJ Woolz's radio station.

That repair beat is where the tape thread pays off mechanically: fixing the Skin Stapler tapes is a named achievement requirement, and the repaired recordings feed the reveals that end the case. If a tape interaction seems stuck, the fix is almost always an unfinished conversation or an unhandled objective in the same room — the [walkthrough](/walkthrough) lists the route's interaction gates in order.

## The orphanage tape: the story's biggest reveal

One recording matters more than the rest. The orphanage audio, delivered in Father Omani's voice, reveals that the killer and his brother were twins separated at birth — one placed with the Ken family and named Silus, the other with the Crow family and named Elias. That single recording converts the taunting serial-killer story into a revenge story aimed at Slater himself.

The tape thread therefore runs through every major beat: a VHS recovered from Luna's crime scene, the tape Scaroline handles at Underworld VHS, the late come-find-me recording, and Slater's own private recording all feed the same reveal chain. The [story guide](/guides/the-skin-stapler-story) lays out the full plot with spoilers, and the [identity page](/guides/who-is-the-skin-stapler) tracks the clue-by-clue reveal.

## Where tape work sits in the chapter route

1. **Investigation chapters.** Slater's CCPD sections open the tape loop — crime-scene recordings enter the case file and the repair tasks begin.
2. **Workplace chapters.** Luna's shop and Scaroline's Underworld VHS tie physical tapes to the story: one becomes evidence from a crime scene, the other makes tape handling a playable job.
3. **The late-game repair session.** The Closing In stretch includes the radio-station visit where the damaged tapes get repaired — completing this work satisfies the Jank Ya Later! achievement requirement and completes the evidence chain before the finale.

Treat tape objectives as mandatory story content, not optional collectibles: the route's progression gates depend on them, and the achievement list confirms at least one unlock is tied directly to the repair task.

## Community claims about the tapes

A fan wiki reports minor epilogue differences based on which tapes you hear — a claim that floats around every horror game with collectible audio. Matched full runs demonstrating different endings have not surfaced; two independent complete playthroughs show the same finale regardless of tape handling. Until someone publishes two matched runs that diverge, treat the single-ending structure as the operative fact and the epilogue-variation claim as an open question.

The same skepticism applies to tape counts. Without an in-game counter or an official number, any total you see online is a player estimate at best — the honest answer is that the game does not surface one.
