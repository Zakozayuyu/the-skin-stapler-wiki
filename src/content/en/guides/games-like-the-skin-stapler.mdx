## Why Sniper Killer is the number-one match

Sniper Killer pairs a serial-killer hunt with mundane job-style tasks and a deliberately ugly PSX aesthetic — the same skeleton The Skin Stapler builds on. Critics reviewing The Skin Stapler reach for it by name, which makes it the safest first purchase for a player who wants the closest thing to another Carrion City case.

The tone matches too: both games play their grotesque material with a straight face and a smirk at the same time, lean on fully voiced dialogue, and wrap a short story around simple interactions. If your favorite part of The Skin Stapler was the investigation chapters, Sniper Killer is the nearest experience; if it was the workplace victim chapters, the Tainted Pact catalog below is the better lane.

## Why Rewind or Die keeps coming up

Rewind or Die shares the retro-video-store DNA that The Skin Stapler wears openly — VHS aesthetics, tape mechanics, and a joke-forward slasher script. The review cites it alongside Sniper Killer as the audience test: fans of either game are the profile that should raise The Skin Stapler's rating by a skull.

It is also the right match for players who liked Scaroline's Underworld VHS chapter most. The store setting, the tape rewinding, and the analog-video humor all point in the same direction.

## The four earlier Tainted Pact horror games

Tainted Pact made The Skin Stapler as a solo developer, and the studio's catalog lists four earlier horror titles, confirmed by both the publisher's release announcement and the GOG catalog:

1. **Suffer the Night** — the studio's analog-horror debut built around a haunted retro computer and a stalking figure from a fictional game.
2. **Terror at Oakheart** — a slasher set at a camp, keeping the short structure and stylized on-screen violence.
3. **Massacre at the Mirage** — a hotel-bound slasher with the same scene-by-scene pacing and dark comedic writing.
4. **Flesh Made Fear** — the most direct predecessor: a serial-killer story with voiced scenes and a grindhouse edge that leads naturally into The Skin Stapler's Carrion City.

Playing them in release order shows the throughline: each game moves closer to the fully voiced, FMV-flavored, viewpoint-switching format The Skin Stapler delivers. The [developer guide](/guides/the-skin-stapler-developer) covers the studio and its publisher in detail.

## What these games share beyond tone

The similarities are structural, not just visual:

- **Short runtimes.** The Skin Stapler runs about 2.5 hours across 14 chapters; the comparison titles are all one-sitting games.
- **Job-sim loops and QTE-style interactions.** Serving hot dogs, filling donuts, rewinding tapes — the mundane-task-as-horror-driver format is the genre's signature.
- **Fully voiced casts.** The Skin Stapler credits ten named performers, and its comparison set also leans on complete voice work rather than text-only dialogue.
- **Synth-heavy retro scores.** Carpenter-style synthesizer themes run through the whole catalog.
- **FMV and video-format flourishes.** VHS-style full-motion video sequences are the presentation glue across these games.
- **Deliberately crude humor.** The publisher's own tagline for The Skin Stapler — expect one last laugh before you die — describes the shared comedic register.
